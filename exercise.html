Guided Breathing Exercise

Follow the circle to practice mindful breathing

This simple yet powerful breathing technique helps calm your mind, reduce stress, and bring you into the present moment. The 4-4-4-4 pattern (breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, breathe out for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds) is known as "box breathing" and is used by athletes, military personnel, and meditation practitioners worldwide.

Body Scan Meditation

Release tension from head to toe

A body scan gently brings awareness to each part of your body, releasing stored tension and stress. Rooted in mindfulness traditions, this practice helps you reconnect with the present moment and cultivates the equanimity that the Gita calls samatvam — balance in all states.

Ready
Focus your attention on each part of your body, noticing any sensations without judgment.

Gratitude Reflection

Cultivate contentment through daily appreciation

The Bhagavad Gita teaches santosha — contentment as a path to peace. Writing down what you are grateful for rewires the mind toward abundance, reducing anxiety and increasing resilience. Even three things a day transforms your perspective over time.

Gita Affirmations

Absorb ancient wisdom one verse at a time

Repetition of sacred truths — called abhyasa in the Gita — gradually transforms the mind. Read each affirmation aloud, pause, breathe, and let it sink in. The Gita says: "For one who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends." (6.6)
